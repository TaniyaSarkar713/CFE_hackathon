Narrative review of comprehensive management strategies for diabetic retinopathy: interdisciplinary approaches and future perspectives
======================================================================================================================================

Xiaoting Pei, Zhijie Li

ABSTRACT
--------
  This review examines the epidemiological trends, pathophysiologic mechanisms, and current and future therapeutic strategies for diabetic retinopathy (DR), focusing on innovative management countermeasures in the face of this global public health challenge. As the number of patients with diabetes continues to increase, DR, as one of its major complications, poses a significant threat to global visual health. This review not only summarises the latest advances in personalised treatment and emerging therapeutic modalities (such as anti-vascular endothelial growth factor therapy, laser treatment, surgical procedures and cutting-edge gene and stem cell therapies) but also emphasises the revolutionary potential of telemedicine technologies and digital health platforms to improve DR screening and adherence among people with diabetes. We show how these technological innovations, especially in resource-limited settings, can achieve early diagnosis and effective treatment, thereby significantly reducing the public health burden of DR. In addition, this article highlights the critical role of interdisciplinary teamwork in optimising the comprehensive management of DR, involving close collaboration among physicians, researchers, patient education specialists and policy-makers, as well as the importance of implementing these innovative solutions through societal engagement and policy support. By highlighting these innovative strategies and their specific impact on improving public health practices, this review offers new perspectives and strategies for the future management of DR, with the goal of promoting the prevention, diagnosis and treatment of DR worldwide, improving patient prognosis and enhancing quality of life.


INTRODUCTION
------------
Diabetic retinopathy (DR), a major complication of diabetes, poses a growing threat to global visual health, with the WHO predicting that the number of patients at risk could increase from approximately 130 million to 160 million by 2045.1 This alarming trend not only underscores DR’s role as the leading cause of blindness in the world’s working-age population but also signals an immediate need for robust, globally applicable DR management strategies.
The variability in DR prevalence and treatment efficacy across global regions further compounds the challenge. In the USA, approximately 7.7 million people with diabetes are affected by DR, including approximately 1.5 million who face vision-threatening conditions.2 Asia, particularly China, reports that 35.1% of its people with diabetes are affected by DR, making it a region with one of the highest numbers of DR patients in the world.3 The situation is even more dire in sub-Saharan Africa, where up to 60% of people with diabetes may suffer from DR due to inadequate diabetes care and management.4 These disparities highlight the complexity of DR as a public health issue and the need for tailored approaches in different regions, especially where resources are scarce.
Although recent research advances have significantly improved the diagnosis and treatment of DR, such as the integration of artificial intelligence (AI) into optical coherence tomography (OCT) and fundus photography, and novel therapies such as fenofibrate and anti-vascular endothelial growth factor (anti-VEGF) treatments, gaps remain in our collective ability to effectively address DR in different healthcare landscapes.5
Figure 1 displays the evolution and future projections of DR.
Given the escalating global burden of DR, there is an urgent need for international collaboration and interdisciplinary research to not only improve community-based screening programmes and public awareness of DR but also to leverage advances in telemedicine and AI to improve access to screening and treatment in underserved areas.
This review attempts to fill the existing gaps in the literature by providing a detailed examination of the epidemiologic trends, pathophysiological mechanisms, current and emerging treatment modalities, and integration of innovative technologies into the public health framework of DR. By shedding light on these areas, we aim to motivate the medical community and the general public towards more effective DR management practices. By emphasising the need for innovative solutions and interdisciplinary collaboration, this article contributes new insights and methodologies for addressing DR, with the ultimate goal of improving disease prevention, treatment efficacy and patient quality of life.

RESEARCH METHODOLOGY
--------------------
This narrative review adhered to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses Extension for Scoping Reviews guidelines. We conducted a systematic search across several prominent databases, including PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science and the Cochrane Library. These databases provide extensive coverage in the fields of biomedical sciences, clinical medicine and public health, encompassing a significant volume of literature on DR, including randomised controlled trials (RCTs), meta-analyses and systematic reviews, thereby capturing the most critical research findings in this domain.
Boolean operators (AND, OR) were employed to combine keywords such as “diabetic retinopathy”, “diabetic macular edema”, “treatment”, “anti-VEGF therapy”, “management”, “telemedicine”, “personalized medicine”, “gene therapy”, “stem cell therapy”, “digital health”, “artificial intelligence”, “multidisciplinary team” and “public health strategy” to ensure a comprehensive search. These terms are recognised for their importance in DR management, reflecting cutting-edge approaches related to technological advancements and are core to understanding and treating DR in clinical practice and research.
Our inclusion criteria prioritised original research articles and reviews published within the last decade, focusing on literature discussing the impact of emerging technologies and interdisciplinary collaboration. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) non-English literature, conference abstracts, commentaries and articles not directly related to the research topic; (2) case reports and studies with small sample sizes (fewer than 50 participants); (3) duplicate publications; (4) studies lacking original data or substantive analysis and (5) articles deemed to be of low quality on quality assessment.
During the literature screening phase, two independent researchers conducted data collection and quality assessment. To evaluate the quality of the studies, the following tools were used based on study design, sample size, data analysis methods, risk of bias and data reliability: (1) For RCTs, we employed the Cochrane Risk of Bias Tool to assess aspects such as selection bias, performance bias, and detection bias; (2) For observational studies, the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale was used to evaluate the selection, comparability and outcome quality of the studies; (3) The quality of systematic reviews and meta-analyses was assessed using a measurement tool to assess systematic reviews (AMSTAR 2) and (4) Narrative reviews were evaluated using the Scale for the Assessment of Narrative Review Articles. In cases where discrepancies arose between the two reviewers’ assessments, a third reviewer was consulted to resolve the differences. Search strategy and flow diagram of study identification and selection are presented in online supplemental file 1.
For literature that met the inclusion criteria, we meticulously extracted key pieces of information, including study design, sample size, intervention measures, main outcomes and conclusions. We then categorised and synthesised this information through content analysis to identify key trends and challenges in DR management. Finally, based on these data, we conducted a comprehensive analysis of current treatment strategies and future research directions, particularly the potential application of innovative technologies and methods in DR management.

PREVENTIVE STRATEGIES FOR DR RISK FACTORS
-----------------------------------------

The importance of lifestyle adjustments and management
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
A healthy lifestyle is pivotal in the prevention of DR, with balanced dietary habits and regular physical activity being fundamental to mitigating DR risk. Maintaining a healthy weight is crucial; being overweight or obese increases insulin resistance, complicating blood sugar control. Conversely, regular exercise and a nutritious diet enhance insulin sensitivity, directly influencing blood sugar and blood pressure management.6 An individualised weight management plan is recommended, reflecting research that associates weight loss with metabolic improvement in people with diabetes and a reduced risk of DR.7
In summary, proactive lifestyle changes, including adopting a healthy diet, increasing physical activity, achieving and maintaining a healthy weight, quitting smoking and moderating alcohol consumption, are essential for people with diabetes. These changes not only facilitate effective blood sugar management but also significantly lower the risk of DR and other diabetes-related complications, thereby markedly enhancing life quality and diminishing the risk of vision loss due to DR.

Innovative strategies related to lifestyle
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
In recent years, personalised health management applications—such as blood glucose monitoring apps and telemedicine platforms—have experienced rapid advancement, offering valuable tools that assist patients in effectively managing their diet, exercise and blood glucose levels. These technologies facilitate self-management by providing real-time data feedback. For instance, studies have demonstrated that mobile health applications can significantly enhance glycaemic control and improve self-management capabilities among individuals with diabetes.8 Furthermore, telemedicine platforms enable patients to receive tailored medical advice and ongoing supervision from the comfort of their homes through regular online consultations and remote monitoring, thereby enhancing adherence to medical recommendations and improving overall management outcomes.9
Wearable devices that track physical activity and blood glucose levels also play a crucial role in promoting adherence to health recommendations, thereby potentially slowing the progression of DR. Devices such as smart bracelets, continuous glucose monitors and smartwatches deliver real-time physiological data, empowering patients to make timely adjustments to their lifestyle and medication regimens.10 Research indicates that individuals with diabetes using continuous glucose monitoring devices achieve significantly better glycaemic control compared with those relying on traditional monitoring methods.11
Emerging personalised dietary adjustment strategies informed by genomics and metabolomics represent a new trend in diabetes management. These approaches analyse individual genetic profiles and metabolic responses to offer customised dietary recommendations, thereby enhancing blood glucose control and preventing the onset of DR. Evidence suggests that personalised nutritional interventions, which take into account genomic and metabolomic data, can markedly improve metabolic health and glycaemic levels in individuals with diabetes.12
In summary, the integration of personalised health management strategies with modern technologies provides individuals with diabetes with more effective means for lifestyle modification. These innovative approaches not only bolster patients’ self-management capabilities but also contribute to mitigating the incidence and progression of diabetes-related complications, particularly DR.

MEDICAL TREATMENT STRATEGIES
----------------------------
Medical treatment strategies for DR include a variety of approaches designed to slow disease progression, improve or maintain vision and prevent severe vision loss. Here is a detailed introduction to treatment strategies for DR:

The importance of early intervention
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Given that the initial stages of DR often do not manifest noticeable visual symptoms, many patients remain unaware of the disease’s presence and progression. Thus, regular retinal examinations are crucial for timely detection and treatment before the disease escalates to more severe stages.13 Early detection and treatment not only control the development of DR but significantly reduce the future need for complex and invasive treatments like laser photocoagulation or vitrectomy.14
The study by Sun et al highlights the necessity for developing pharmaceutical interventions aimed at early treatment based on an understanding of specific mechanisms such as leucocyte adhesion and microvascular abnormalities.15 Furthermore, educational interventions have been shown to significantly improve the knowledge and awareness of patients with DR, leading to better disease management and outcomes. Safaan et al demonstrated that a nursing-led educational intervention could markedly enhance patients' understanding of their condition and the importance of early intervention.16
In recent years, AI technology, particularly deep learning algorithms, has gained prominence in the automated analysis of retinal and OCT images. These AI-driven diagnostic tools enable rapid and efficient detection of early DR lesions, yielding highly accurate diagnostic results. Notably, in resource-limited areas, these technologies significantly improve the early detection rate of DR through telemedicine platforms.17
Regarding imaging technologies, novel ultra-widefield imaging systems, such as the Optos ultra-widefield imaging system, facilitate the capture of a broader area of the retina. This capability allows clinicians to identify peripheral lesions that may be overlooked in conventional retinal examinations. These advancements not only promote earlier detection of DR but also offer a more comprehensive understanding of disease progression.18 19
Multispectral imaging represents an emerging non-invasive imaging modality that captures retinal images across various spectra, enabling the early identification of microvascular lesions, retinal haemorrhages and other early manifestations of DR. This technology is adept at detecting subtle vascular changes in the fundus, thereby facilitating timely intervention.20 Additionally, OCT angiography is a novel imaging tool that operates without the need for contrast agents, providing high-resolution visualisation of the microstructure of retinal vessels. This capability allows for the detection of microvascular changes and non-perfusion areas, thereby identifying DR microvascular abnormalities earlier than traditional OCT methods.21
Furthermore, the next generation of non-contact imaging devices, such as handheld fundus cameras and smartphone attachments, is becoming increasingly portable and user-friendly, enabling large-scale screening in resource-limited settings.22 The advancements in these technologies significantly contribute to the early detection and intervention of DR, ultimately improving patients’ visual prognosis.

Anti-VEGF therapy
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Anti-VEGF therapy has emerged as a pivotal advancement in treating DR, especially diabetic macular oedema (DME) and proliferative DR (PDR). VEGF plays a significant role in promoting the formation and permeability of blood vessels under both physiological and pathological conditions. In DR, excessive VEGF release leads to the proliferation of abnormal blood vessels and leakage from existing vessels, culminating in vision loss.23
Figure 2 displays the pathophysiological mechanisms of DR. The introduction of anti-VEGF therapy offers an effective treatment option for DR, directly targeting the disease’s underlying vascular abnormalities. Through intraocular injections of anti-VEGF drugs, significant reductions in retinal VEGF levels are achieved, thereby decreasing vascular leakage and inhibiting abnormal neovascularisation, ultimately improving or stabilising vision.23 24
Currently available anti-VEGF drugs for DR treatment include bevacizumab (Avastin), ranibizumab (Lucentis) and aflibercept (Eylea), each varying in molecular structure and pharmacokinetic properties but showing substantial efficacy in reducing DME and controlling PDR.23 25 Ultra-widefield fluorescein angiography has improved the characterisation of peripheral lesions in DR, aiding in the identification of patients at risk for advanced disease stages. Protocols from DRCR Retina Network have demonstrated the efficacy of anti-VEGF treatment in managing select PDR patients, particularly those without high-risk features, suggesting a potential shift in treatment paradigms.26
Faricimab represents a significant advancement in the management of DR as the first bispecific antibody designed to concurrently target both the VEGF-A and angiopoietin-2 (Ang-2) pathways. Unlike traditional anti-VEGF therapies, faricimab operates through a dual mechanism that not only reduces neovascularisation but also diminishes vascular leakage. This innovative approach has the potential to enhance therapeutic efficacy and extend the intervals between injections, thereby improving patient compliance. Clinical trials, notably the YOSEMITE and RHINE studies, have provided compelling evidence supporting the superiority of faricimab in prolonging treatment intervals, decreasing retinal oedema and enhancing visual acuity.27 28 As the adoption of anti-VEGF therapies becomes more prevalent, biosimilars—such as those derived from ranibizumab and bevacizumab—are progressively entering the market. These biosimilars offer more competitively priced alternatives, which are anticipated to improve the accessibility of anti-VEGF treatments, particularly in resource-limited settings, thereby facilitating a more effective global response to DR management.29 Furthermore, the development of small extracellular vesicle-loaded bevacizumab introduces a novel strategy aimed at potentially reducing the frequency of intravitreal injections required for DR treatment. This innovative approach not only holds promise for enhancing patient comfort but also warrants further investigation to evaluate its efficacy in clinical settings.30
As the therapeutic landscape for DR continues to evolve, the promise of new anti-VEGF drugs and enhanced administration strategies herald more effective and personalised treatment options for patients, alongside ongoing studies into long-term efficacy and safety to optimise treatment protocols.31 32

Laser photocoagulation and surgical intervention
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Laser photocoagulation and surgical interventions remain integral in treating DR, especially for patients who do not fully respond to or are ineligible for anti-VEGF therapy.33 Despite the advent and growing utilisation of anti-VEGF medications, these traditional modalities are indispensable for managing specific DR types, serving as vital tools in the ophthalmologist’s repertoire for combating severe vision loss due to DR.
Laser photocoagulation targets PDR and select cases of DME.34 35 By applying precise laser beams, it aims to shrink and seal off abnormal blood vessels, mitigating retinal leakage and halting disease progression. Meanwhile, vitrectomy is reserved for advanced DR stages, especially when vitreous haemorrhage or retinal detachment occurs, allowing for the removal of blood and scar tissue from the retina and vitreous and treating abnormal neovascularisation, often combined with intraocular tamponades to support retinal reattachment.
Recent studies have further refined our understanding and application of these interventions. A systematic review by Imani highlighted the effectiveness of anti-VEGF therapy compared with pan-retinal photocoagulation laser therapy in patients with PDR, suggesting a more favourable outcome in visual acuity with anti-VEGF treatments.36 Conversely, laser photocoagulation combined with anti-VEGF drugs, such as ranibizumab, has been shown to significantly improve outcomes in DR by effectively improving neovascularisation and reducing VEGF levels, thus controlling further progression of lesions.37
Targeted retinal photocoagulation emerges as a promising advancement, focusing specifically on unperfused retinal capillaries and intermediate ischaemic areas, potentially reducing tissue perfusion area damage and complications associated with panretinal photocoagulation.38 This approach, especially when combined with intravitreal injection of anti-VEGF drugs, may offer a new therapeutic pathway for DR treatment, suggesting a potential shift towards more targeted and less damaging laser treatments in the future.
The selection between laser treatment and surgical intervention should be based on a comprehensive patient condition assessment, including DR type, lesion extent and overall health status.34 39 The comparative analysis of treatment strategies for DR is shown in table 1. Sometimes, a combination of traditional treatment methods with anti-VEGF therapy is necessary for optimal therapeutic outcomes.
As technology evolves and new therapeutic strategies emerge, the application of laser treatment and surgical intervention in DR management is expected to become more personalised and precise, underscoring the importance of keeping abreast with the latest clinical research and technological advancements for devising the most suitable treatment plans for patients.
Furthermore, interdisciplinary collaboration is crucial for enhancing treatment outcomes and patient satisfaction, highlighting the dynamic and evolving nature of DR management strategies.

New strategies for the treatment of diabetes retinopathy
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Recent advancements in the management of DR have highlighted several innovative therapeutic approaches. One such approach involves the use of receptor-interacting protein kinase 1 (RIP1 kinase) inhibitors. These agents target necroptotic cell death pathways in the retina, offering a potential strategy to prevent retinal damage. Emerging evidence from animal studies demonstrates that RIP1 kinase inhibitors possess significant neuroprotective effects, suggesting their promise for future clinical applications in DR management.40
Gene therapy has also emerged as a revolutionary treatment modality, particularly for patients with refractory DR.41 This innovative approach uses gene editing technologies, such as CRISPR-Cas9, as well as gene transfer therapies, to repair genetic mutations or regulate abnormal angiogenesis within the retina. Although gene therapy remains in the nascent stages of research, preliminary findings indicate substantial potential, providing new hope for the future management of DR.42 43
In the realm of regenerative medicine, stem cell therapy is being explored as a viable treatment for DR. Transplantation of retinal pigment epithelial cells and neural stem cells aims to repair damaged retinal tissue and restore visual function. Recent studies have reported the efficacy of stem cell therapy in both animal models and early-phase clinical trials, expanding the therapeutic options available for DR patients.42 44
Sodium-dependent glucose transporter 2 (SGLT2) inhibitors, primarily used to manage hyperglycaemia and associated conditions such as hypertension and chronic heart failure, have shown promise in the context of DR.42 Recent research indicates that SGLT2 inhibitors can mitigate peripheral cell swelling, delay vascular alterations and may be particularly beneficial for early-stage DR. For instance, dapagliflozin, an SGLT2 inhibitor, has been identified as playing a critical role in reducing retinal capillary blood flow and preventing vascular congestion.45
Additionally, emerging hormonal therapies targeting retinal vascular leakage are under development. Studies have demonstrated that agents such as fenofibrate offer protective effects against DR. Furthermore, molecular targets like Ang-2 inhibitors have the potential to modulate vascular homeostasis through various pathways, reducing neovascularisation and alleviating macular oedema, thereby highlighting their promising therapeutic potential.27 28

PATIENT MANAGEMENT AND EDUCATION
--------------------------------
Patient management and education play a key role in the comprehensive management of DR.16 Patient management and education strategies are shown in figure 3. The following is a detailed introduction to this aspect.

The importance of patient self-management education
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Patient self-management education is critical for controlling diabetes and its complications, including DR.46 47 This includes providing knowledge on how to monitor and control blood glucose levels, understanding how diet and exercise affect blood glucose levels, and managing daily life with diabetes.48 This educational process aims to deepen patients’ understanding of diabetes and its complications, especially how to effectively monitor and control blood glucose levels and the impact of diet and exercise on glycaemic control.46 Through such education, patients’ self-management skills can be improved, reducing the risk of disease progression and improving quality of life.

Recommendations for regular eye examinations and screening
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
In managing DR, equipping patients with the ability to recognise early symptoms and seek professional medical help when necessary is critical in preventing severe vision loss. Highlighting the importance of regular eye examinations is essential, as it aids in the early detection and intervention of DR, thereby potentially halting or slowing the disease’s progression.49 50
Moreover, effective patient education goes beyond merely imparting disease knowledge; it critically aims at enhancing patients’ awareness and their self-management capabilities.48 This encompasses, among other practices, the establishment of healthy lifestyle habits, such as maintaining a balanced diet and engaging in moderate exercise, as well as the correct use of medication.51 By improving patients’ self-management skills, the risk of DR and other diabetes complications can be significantly reduced, thereby improving the patients’ quality of life.
Recent studies underscore the efficacy of educational interventions in enhancing self-care management and knowledge among patients with DR. For instance, an educational intervention study by Safaan et al demonstrated significant improvement in self-care practices among patients with DR, emphasising the vital role of such interventions in patient care.16
Another study by Mostafa evaluated the effectiveness of an educational programme in improving the knowledge of critically ill patients with DR, further highlighting the critical role of health education in managing this condition.52
These findings advocate for the integration of structured educational programmes into DR management strategies, aiming to enhance patient knowledge, self-care practices, and ultimately, quality of life. This approach not only addresses the immediate needs of patients with DR but also aligns with broader objectives of diabetes management, emphasising the importance of patient education and empowerment in chronic disease management.

Patient support and resources
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
In managing DR, it is essential to provide patients with comprehensive support and resources. These include patient support groups, educational materials, online resources and specialised diabetes management teams, all aimed at delivering detailed information and guidance on daily disease management, available treatment options and recommended lifestyle adjustments.53 54
Patient support groups are invaluable, offering psychological and emotional support that helps patients understand and manage their condition, share experiences and gain treatment insights. This support significantly alleviates the psychological burden and feelings of isolation, fostering a positive attitude towards disease management challenges.55
Educational materials and online resources grant patients access to the latest research findings, treatment methods and management strategies. Designed to be easily understandable and cater to patients’ needs, these resources empower patients to make informed medical decisions and enhance their self-management capabilities.56
Specialised diabetes care teams, comprising endocrinologists, ophthalmologists, diabetes education nurses, dietitians and others, provide personalised medical services. They develop comprehensive treatment plans and management advice tailored to each patient’s specific conditions, ensuring the best possible treatment and support from multiple perspectives.57
Recent studies have highlighted the importance of support mechanisms in the management of DR, underscoring the pivotal role of education and personalised care in improving patient outcomes. Safaan et al demonstrated that educational interventions can significantly improve self-care practices in patients with DR, emphasising the critical role of education in patient care.16 Similarly, Kumar et al investigated the perceptions of patients referred for management of sight-threatening DR in India, highlighting the urgent need for improved health literacy and implementation of effective patient education strategies.58 In another study, Pandit et al focused on tailoring treatment approaches to the individual needs of patients with PDR, highlighting the importance of personalised care in the treatment regimen.26 Furthermore, Vishva et al provided a comprehensive review of the risk factors, diagnostic procedures and management strategies for DR, underscoring the critical need for early detection, timely intervention and proactive management of risk factors to mitigate disease progression.59 Collectively, these findings support the integration of structured educational programmes and personalised treatment plans into DR management strategies to improve patient knowledge, self-care practices, and ultimately, quality of life. Patient education strategies and practical examples in the management of DR
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Effective education strategies and tools are crucial in DR management, significantly improving patient self-management skills and outcomes. Here, we delve into successful patient education programmes and practical tools that have shown to enhance patient understanding and management of DR:

    Digital health platforms
Digital health platforms such as RetinaGo offer an interactive learning environment for people with diabetes through videos, graphics and interactive quizzes about DR. These platforms tailor educational content to the user’s progression and comprehension level, effectively boosting patient awareness about managing DR.49

    Community health lectures
Community centres and hospitals host health lectures, such as the Bright Vision Programme, to educate patients. These lectures and workshops cover healthy eating, blood glucose control and DR prevention, led by ophthalmologists and dietitians. They aim to increase patient awareness and self-management skills, involving DR patients and their families.16

    Patient support groups
Support groups such as Vision Guardians offer a space for DR patients to exchange experiences and receive support. Regular meetings enable patients to learn coping strategies, access new information and manage disease challenges more effectively. These groups discuss the latest DR management research and lifestyle modifications through online and offline meetings.13

    Customised patient education materials
Customised materials such as brochures, infographics and reminder cards play a vital role in aiding patients’ understanding of DR treatment and self-management. These materials, encompassing disease knowledge, lifestyle modifications, medication guidelines and FAQs, are tailored to meet diverse patient needs.54
Recent research highlights the significant impact of educational initiatives on DR management. Naji and Abed’s study illustrates that structured education programmes can substantially enhance diabetic patients’ understanding of retinopathy prevention, reinforcing the importance of patient awareness and the adoption of preventive behaviours.60 Similarly, Safaan et al demonstrated that educational interventions significantly improve self-care management among patients with retinopathy, underscoring the benefits of personalised education for better self-management in DR patients.16 Palar et al further support these findings by showing that a well-organised teaching programme can greatly increase diabetic patients’ knowledge regarding DR prevention, emphasising the effectiveness of structured educational content in elevating patient understanding and awareness about disease management.61 Moreover, the utilisation of virtual reality (VR) technology in the VISION RELIEF initiative has notably enhanced the quality of patient care, by providing vivid illustrations of retinopathy essentials, which in turn improves learning outcomes and promotes better screening and multidisciplinary management practices.62 These examples underscore the critical need for ongoing development of educational programmes that are tailored to the specific needs of DR patients, ensuring the content is both relevant and practical. By leveraging innovative teaching methods and technology, these programmes can significantly enhance patient understanding and management of DR, ultimately contributing to improved treatment adherence and a better quality of life.

DR MANAGEMENT CHALLENGES AND SSTRATEGIES
----------------------------------------
Challenges and strategies in the management of DR constitute a complex issue involving socioeconomic factors, healthcare resource allocation, healthcare team collaboration and patient education. Here is a detailed introduction to this part:

Impact of socioeconomic factors on diabetes retinopathy management
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Socioeconomic factors play an undeniable role in the management of DR. Studies indicate that individuals from lower socioeconomic backgrounds face significant challenges in accessing quality diabetes and DR-related healthcare services.63 These challenges include, but are not limited to, financial burden, access to transportation and limited access to knowledge and information about healthcare services. Economic hardship may cause patients to delay seeking medical care, missing the critical window for early diagnosis and treatment, thereby increasing the risk of DR progressing to more severe stages.64
Furthermore, groups with lower socioeconomic status often have lower levels of health awareness and education, which may further limit their understanding of the importance of disease prevention and management.65 66 This lack of awareness not only affects patients’ willingness to proactively seek medical services but may also affect their ability to adhere to treatment plans.
To address these issues, the implementation of comprehensive strategies is critical. First, governments and relevant health organisations should implement medical assistance programmes targeted at low-income populations to reduce their financial burden and increase their likelihood of undergoing eye examinations and treatment.67 Second, improving the level of public health education, especially in low-income communities, through community health activities, educational seminars and media campaigns can increase people’s awareness of diabetes and DR and the importance of early detection and treatment.50
Finally, the use of innovative measures such as mobile health services through temporary eye clinics or ‘medical buses’ can bring health resources directly to remote or resource-limited communities, thereby improving the accessibility and convenience of medical services.68 These interventions can not only help individuals from lower socioeconomic backgrounds access needed health services but can also effectively reduce the risk of vision loss and blindness caused by DR.
In conclusion, the impact of socioeconomic factors on the management of DR is profound, requiring efforts in policy support, educational improvement and service innovation to provide equitable, high-quality medical services to all patients with diabetes. Such comprehensive strategies can not only improve disease management and quality of life for patients but also have a positive impact on the entire public health system.

The importance of multidisciplinary team collaboration
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
In the management of DR, adopting a multidisciplinary team (MDT) collaboration model is considered a key strategy to improve treatment outcomes and patient satisfaction.69 70 This collaboration model fosters close cooperation among professionals from various fields, such as endocrinology, ophthalmology, nutrition, mental health and social work, offering patients comprehensive and personalised treatment plans. The core advantage of an MDT lies in its ability to assess the patient’s condition from multiple perspectives, creating treatment plans that meet the patient’s specific needs, covering comprehensive management from blood sugar control to vision protection and quality of life improvement.71
The effective operation of an MDT relies on regular communication and information sharing among team members. This not only helps to promptly identify and resolve issues encountered during the treatment process but also allows the team to adjust treatment plans flexibly based on changes in the patient’s condition. Additionally, each team member can provide professional guidance to patients based on their expertise, such as dietary advice from nutritionists, psychological support from mental health professionals, and assistance with social and economic issues from social workers, all working together to ensure patients receive comprehensive medical care.72
Another crucial aspect of the MDT collaboration model is patient education. Through the joint efforts of the MDT, patients can gain comprehensive knowledge about disease management, medication treatments, and adjustments in daily life, thereby enhancing their self-management capabilities and reducing the risk of disease recurrence and complications. Patient education also includes encouraging patients to participate in treatment decisions, enhancing their understanding and compliance with the treatment plan, ultimately improving treatment outcomes.73
In summary, the role of MDTs in DR management is indispensable. By promoting collaboration and information sharing across professional fields, MDTs not only provide patients with personalised and comprehensive treatment plans but also improve the overall treatment experience and quality of life through patient education and support. As the medical model continues to evolve, MDT collaboration will play an increasingly important role in the management of DR and other chronic diseases.

Strategies to improve patient adherence
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
In the management of DR, improving patient adherence to treatment plans is one of the key factors in achieving treatment success.74 Unfortunately, many patients fail to adhere to treatment recommendations for a variety of reasons, including the cost of treatment, concerns about potential side effects, the complexity of the treatment regimen or simply forgetting to take medications and attend medical appointments.75 To overcome these challenges, the healthcare team must employ a multidimensional strategy to ensure that patients understand, accept and adhere to the treatment plan.
First, patient education is the cornerstone of improving adherence. By thoroughly explaining the nature of the disease, the importance of the treatment plan and the risks associated with not following the treatment regimen, patients can better understand why they need to follow medical advice.76 Doctors and nurses should use language that is easily understood by patients and avoid overly technical terms to ensure transparency and accessibility of information.
Second, the use of reminder services, such as mobile apps or text message reminders, can effectively help patients remember medication times and follow-up appointments. These technological methods are becoming increasingly popular in modern healthcare management, providing patients with convenient and real-time reminders, thus reducing the problems of non-adherence due to forgetfulness.77
In addition, simplifying the treatment regimen is also an effective way to improve adherence. Reducing the number of daily doses or using long-acting medications to decrease the frequency of injections can significantly reduce the patient’s treatment burden, thereby increasing their willingness and ability to follow the treatment plan.14
Finally, providing psychological support is equally important in enhancing patients’ self-management skills and adherence. Faced with the stress and uncertainty of a chronic disease, patients may feel anxious, depressed or isolated. The healthcare team should provide adequate emotional support to help patients develop positive coping mechanisms, encourage active participation in treatment and maintain an optimistic attitude.78

Measures and innovative solutions to overcome the management challenges of diabetes retinopathy
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
The challenges of managing DR, especially in resource-limited settings, make the adoption of innovative strategies and solutions critical. The use of telemedicine technologies, such as smartphone fundus cameras and remote diagnostic platforms, provides an effective means to extend the reach of medical services and ensure that patients in remote areas have access to timely DR screening and diagnostic services.79 80 This technology, which uses digital fundus photography and AI algorithms, efficiently identifies early signs of DR and implements timely interventions, not only increasing the efficiency and accuracy of screenings but also reducing barriers to accessing medical services due to transportation, time or financial factors.79 81 The introduction of remote education tools further enhances the knowledge and skills of healthcare providers while increasing patient awareness of DR.81 Although telemedicine has significant advantages in increasing screening rates and access to care, its implementation still faces technical, legal and ethical challenges, such as ensuring adequate bandwidth, data security and addressing legal regulations regarding patient privacy and cross-border medical services, which are key barriers that must be overcome to promote telemedicine technologies.
At the community level, establishing a patient-centred DR management model that trains community health workers and volunteers in preliminary screening and basic education can effectively increase patient awareness of early detection and management of DR while reducing reliance on advanced health facilities.80 This model promotes community engagement and strengthens the capacity for early detection and management of DR and is particularly suitable for resource-limited settings.
In terms of cost, the development of economically viable treatment methods and devices, such as low-cost drug delivery systems and intravitreal injection devices, enables the widespread use of advanced treatments such as anti-VEGF therapy in resource-limited settings.82 In addition, interdisciplinary collaboration and knowledge sharing play a key role in DR management. Collaboration between ophthalmologists, diabetes specialists, basic researchers and public health experts facilitates the integration of expertise from different fields to explore and implement optimal treatment strategies.81
Policy support and funding from governments and non-governmental organisations are essential to promote research and implementation of innovative solutions in DR management. Funding for remote medical projects, community health programmes and infrastructure construction can not only improve DR treatment outcomes but also improve patients’ quality of life. In addition, encouraging private sector investment in health technology development can further promote innovation and optimisation of DR treatment methods.
In summary, the above-mentioned efforts and innovative strategies can significantly improve the efficiency and effectiveness of DR management, even in resource-limited settings, and offer a brighter future for patients with DR. This will require the joint participation and continued efforts of medical professionals, policy-makers and all sectors of society to ensure that technological advances benefit all patients.

Interdisciplinary management
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

    Telemedicine and digital platforms for early detection and continuous monitoring
In the landscape of DR management, telemedicine and digital platforms represent a transformative shift towards more accessible, efficient and patient-centred healthcare.83 This paradigm shift is largely driven by advances in digital health technologies, including high-resolution retinal imaging and sophisticated data analytics, which have enabled remote diagnosis and monitoring of DR with a level of precision previously only available in clinical settings.84
A key component of these digital platforms is their ability to seamlessly integrate with existing healthcare infrastructures, enabling the smooth transfer of retinal images and patient data between remote primary care providers and specialised ophthalmology centres.85 This integration not only facilitates early detection of DR by expanding access to specialised diagnostic tools but also ensures that patients receive timely referrals for treatment, thereby preventing progression of the disease to more severe stages.86
In addition, these platforms often include educational modules tailored to the needs of patients with diabetes. Through the use of multimedia resources and interactive tools, patients can gain a deeper understanding of their condition, learn about the importance of regular eye exams and become more engaged in their care plans.87 This educational component is critical as it empowers patients to take proactive steps to manage their diabetes and maintain their eye health, further contributing to positive outcomes.
The continuous monitoring capabilities of these digital platforms also play a crucial role in the management of DR. Through wearable devices or smartphone applications, patients can regularly capture retinal images, which are then automatically analysed by AI algorithms for signs of disease progression.88 This real-time monitoring allows healthcare providers to adjust treatment plans as needed, ensuring that interventions are as effective as possible. Additionally, these platforms can send reminders to patients for medication adherence and scheduled eye exams, further supporting patient engagement and treatment compliance.
Furthermore, the data collected by these digital platforms offer invaluable insights into the epidemiology of DR, helping researchers and public health officials better understand patterns of disease occurrence and progression. These data can inform the development of targeted public health interventions and contribute to the ongoing improvement of DR management strategies.
In conclusion, the advent of telemedicine and digital platforms for the early detection and continuous monitoring of DR signifies a promising advance in the fight against this vision-threatening complication of diabetes. By bridging the gap between remote patients and healthcare providers, enhancing patient education and engagement and enabling real-time disease monitoring, these digital solutions are setting a new standard for comprehensive, patient-centric care in the realm of diabetic eye disease.89 As these technologies continue to evolve and become more widely adopted, they hold the potential to significantly reduce the global burden of DR, transforming the lives of millions of individuals living with diabetes.

    Multidisciplinary diabetes care teams for comprehensive management
The innovative approach of forming multidisciplinary diabetes care teams that include diabetologists, ophthalmologists, diabetes educators, nutritionists and mental health professionals has significantly improved the management of DR by providing a holistic model of care.90 These teams bring together a variety of healthcare professionals to provide comprehensive care, targeting the various aspects that contribute to the progression of DR. Diabetologists are critical in achieving optimal glycaemic control through tailored medication regimens, which directly impact the risk factors associated with DR. Ophthalmologists contribute their expertise by focusing on early detection and treatment of DR, using advanced imaging techniques to monitor changes in retinal health and initiate timely interventions.91 Diabetes educators bridge the knowledge gap for patients, empowering them with the skills necessary to effectively manage their condition and emphasising the importance of regular eye exams. Dietitians play a key role by providing individualised nutrition counselling to help patients manage their blood glucose levels through diet, which is an essential component of overall diabetes care. In addition, mental health professionals address the psychological and emotional challenges associated with managing diabetes and potential vision loss, providing support that is critical to patient adherence to treatment plans.
This multidisciplinary approach not only optimises medical treatment but also improves patient adherence to lifestyle changes, ultimately leading to better health outcomes. The team’s collaborative efforts ensure that the patient’s educational, nutritional, psychological and medical needs are met, significantly reducing risk factors for DR progression such as poor glycaemic control. In addition, this comprehensive care model creates a supportive environment that encourages patients to be active participants in their care, which is critical to managing a complex condition like diabetes and its complications.
Looking to the future, the integration of telehealth and digital health tools into these MDTs opens new avenues for improving care. Technologies such as remote monitoring and digital communication platforms could further increase the efficiency and accessibility of patient–provider interactions, enabling closer disease management and timely interventions.92 The potential to use AI and machine learning to predict DR risk and tailor treatment plans underscores the evolving nature of these care models, which incorporate technological advances to meet the growing needs of patients.53

    Patient education programmes tailored by Interdisciplinary teams
Interdisciplinary teams, leveraging the synergy between healthcare professionals, IT experts and patient advocates, have innovatively crafted patient education programmes that markedly amplify patient comprehension and management of DR. The cornerstone of these programmes lies in their utilisation of cutting-edge technologies, including mobile applications,93 VR simulations62 and AI-powered chatbots,94 which collectively foster a more interactive and engaging learning environment for patients.

      Mobile applications
These apps offer personalised guidance and reminders for medication intake, appointment schedules and eye examination reminders.95 By embedding educational content directly into the user interface, patients receive real-time feedback and support, enabling them to make informed decisions about their health. Moreover, some apps integrate blood sugar and blood pressure tracking functionalities, providing a holistic view of the patient’s health status, which is crucial for managing conditions like DR.

      VR simulations
VR technology immerses patients in a three-dimensional environment where they can visualise the impacts of DR on the eye, offering a profound understanding of the disease’s progression and the importance of early intervention.62 These simulations can also guide patients through the procedural aspects of treatment options, reducing anxiety and building confidence in the care process. By simulating real-life scenarios, patients can better grasp the significance of adherence to treatment plans and lifestyle modifications.

      AI-powered chatbots
Chatbots serve as 24/7 virtual assistants, offering instant responses to patient inquiries regarding DR management, dietary recommendations and symptom tracking.94 These intelligent systems learn from interactions to provide tailored advice, making the management of DR more accessible. The chatbots can also nudge patients towards healthier habits, play a pivotal role in dispelling myths and misinformation and offer psychological support, thereby enhancing the overall management of the condition.
The collaborative design of these education programmes ensures they are comprehensive, catering to the diverse needs of the patient population. By incorporating feedback from healthcare professionals and patients alike, the programmes are continuously refined to improve usability and effectiveness. This patient-centred approach has led to significant advancements in patient engagement, empowering individuals with the knowledge and tools necessary for effective self-management. As patients become more involved in their care, adherence to treatment plans improves, ultimately leading to enhanced disease outcomes and a higher quality of life.
These education programmes exemplify the potential of interdisciplinary collaboration in transforming patient care. By harnessing the power of technology and tailored educational content, these programmes offer a proactive approach to managing DR, highlighting the evolving landscape of patient education in chronic disease management. As technology advances, these programmes will continue to play a crucial role in empowering patients, fostering an environment where informed decisions and active participation in care lead to better health outcomes.

FUTURE DIRECTIONS AND RESEARCH PROSPECTS
----------------------------------------
In the field of medical management of DR, ongoing innovation and research continue to break new ground in treatment modalities, personalised medical strategies and the use of technological advances to improve the efficiency of patient management. Here are some important future directions in this field:

Research advances in new treatments
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
As medical research deepens, the treatment landscape for DR is rapidly evolving, particularly in the exploration of new therapeutic approaches. These aim not only to control disease progression but also to address the underlying causes of the disease, thereby improving patients’ potential for visual recovery and quality of life. Scientists are exploring multiple treatment targets based on the pathological mechanisms of DR. In addition to the widely used anti-VEGF therapies, new research is focusing on key biological pathways such as fibroblast growth factor, platelet-derived growth factor and the Wnt/β-catenin pathway. These are aimed at reducing retinal vascular leakage and abnormal neovascularisation to comprehensively address the visual impairment caused by DR.96 97 Gene therapy and stem cell therapy, as revolutionary fields for the treatment of DR, show immense potential. The former aims to achieve curative treatment by correcting pathogenic genes, while the latter harnesses the regenerative potential of stem cells to repair damaged retinal cells.98 99 However, the safety, efficacy and long-term effects of these treatments need to be validated through extensive clinical trials. Personalised medicine plays an increasingly important role in selecting treatment strategies, which should be optimised based on the patient’s genetic background, disease state and personal needs. In summary, emerging treatment methods for DR are opening new doors for more in-depth and personalised treatment. As scientific research progresses, the future promises more effective, safe and personalised treatment options for DR patients.

Personalised medicine in diabetes retinopathy management
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Personalised medicine, often referred to as precision medicine, customises treatment plans based on an individual’s genetic profile, lifestyle and specific disease characteristics. In the context of DR management, this approach leverages genetic markers to assess the risk of disease progression and predict responses to treatment, thereby facilitating more tailored and effective therapeutic strategies for patients.100 By adjusting drug dosages and treatment schedules according to a patient’s unique disease state and responses, personalised medicine aims to maximise treatment efficacy while minimising adverse effects.101
The integration of personalised medicine into DR management represents a significant advancement in medical treatment, particularly within the framework of precision medicine. By considering individual genetic backgrounds, biomarkers, environmental factors and lifestyle choices, customised treatment strategies can be developed for each DR patient.
Current research has identified several genetic markers that correlate with both the risk of developing DR and the efficacy of various treatments. Notably, specific genetic variations, such as polymorphisms in the VEGF gene, have been associated with disease progression and response to anti-VEGF therapies. Genetic analysis can effectively identify patients more likely to benefit from anti-VEGF treatments, enabling the formulation of personalised treatment regimens.102 Moreover, strategies that target inflammatory and oxidative stress pathways are being explored, reflecting the latest advancements in understanding these mechanisms.103
Personalised treatment extends beyond genetic considerations; it also encompasses tailored plans based on individual patient circumstances, including the duration of diabetes, the presence of comorbidities and lifestyle factors. For example, individualised management of glycosylated hemoglobin, type A1C (HbA1c) levels, blood pressure and lipid profiles is essential in the context of DR. These clinical characteristics significantly influence the selection of optimal treatment modalities, such as the frequency of anti-VEGF injections, the incorporation of combination therapies (eg, laser photocoagulation alongside pharmacotherapy) and lifestyle modifications.41
Adjustments to the dosage and injection intervals of anti-VEGF therapy are critical, based on the patient’s response to treatment. While some patients may achieve satisfactory outcomes with standard dosing regimens, others might require more frequent injections or higher doses to effectively manage disease progression.104 Continuous monitoring of disease status through repeated retinal imaging techniques, such as OCT, is vital for optimising individualised treatment plans.
In conclusion, the application of personalised medicine in the management of DR highlights the potential of precision medicine to improve treatment outcomes and enhance patient experiences. As research continues to advance and technologies evolve, personalised medicine is poised to play an increasingly pivotal role in the management of DR and other related conditions, ultimately striving to preserve vision and enhance quality of life for patients.14

Technological innovations and the potential of telemedicine
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
With the rapid advancement of technology, telemedicine has become an important innovation in the management of DR. In particular, with the support of digital health technologies and AI, unprecedented opportunities have been opened up for early screening, monitoring and intervention of DR.105 106 These technologies not only meet patients’ demands for convenient and efficient medical services but also open new avenues for healthcare providers to deliver high-quality eye care in resource-limited settings.

    Digital retinal imaging technology
Digital retinal imaging technology enables the remote transmission of retinal photographs, allowing doctors to assess patients without direct face-to-face interaction. The use of this technology significantly increases the efficiency of DR screening programmes, allowing patients in remote areas to access timely DR screening services.107 Compared with traditional fundus examination, digital retinal imaging technology provides higher-quality images, which helps clinicians to more accurately analyse and diagnose lesions.

    Application of AI in diabetes retinopathy diagnosis
AI, particularly through the use of deep learning algorithms, has demonstrated remarkable potential in the automatic detection, classification and analysis of DR images.108 109 These AI systems can efficiently process vast quantities of retinal image data, accurately identifying subtle lesions that may elude even the most experienced clinicians. This capability positions AI as a crucial adjunctive tool, enabling ophthalmologists to detect DR at earlier stages and facilitating timely intervention.
When comparing AI to traditional screening methods, such as manual assessments conducted by ophthalmologists, AI has shown superior sensitivity, specificity, speed and scalability. Numerous studies indicate that AI technologies have achieved diagnostic accuracies comparable to, or exceeding, those of certain human experts.81 110 This advancement underscores the value of AI in enhancing the effectiveness and efficiency of DR screening programmes.
Despite the promising applications of AI in clinical settings, several challenges must be addressed, including concerns regarding data privacy, algorithmic bias, regulatory compliance and the need for widespread adoption in practical environments.111 Tackling these issues is essential for the successful integration of AI into routine clinical practice. Moreover, the future trajectory of AI in DR management will likely involve greater integration with telemedicine platforms, thereby providing convenient DR screening services for patients in remote or underserved regions.
In summary, the incorporation of digital retinal imaging technologies alongside AI in the management of DR holds significant promise for enhancing early detection and intervention, especially in areas with limited access to healthcare. As these technologies advance and existing challenges are systematically addressed, they are expected to revolutionise the landscape of DR care, delivering more personalised and accessible healthcare solutions.

    The potential of telehealth services
The potential of telehealth services in managing DR is increasingly recognised as a pivotal component in enhancing patient care. Telemedicine services, facilitating online consultations, disease management advice and patient education, significantly bolster the self-management capacities of DR patients, offering a blend of convenience, efficiency and personalised care.112 These services not only mitigate the time and economic burdens on patients but also foster their active engagement and satisfaction in managing their condition. Furthermore, telemedicine’s capability for continuous disease monitoring emerges as a critical benefit, especially for those hindered by geographical remoteness or physical limitations from regular hospital visits.
Recent advancements further extend the scope and efficacy of telehealth in DR management. The third edition of the Practice Guidelines for Ocular Telehealth-Diabetic Retinopathy underscores an evolving framework to refine telehealth’s clinical applications in ophthalmology, emphasising standardised protocols for image acquisition and evaluation.113 Concurrently, the integration of AI within telehealth frameworks is poised to revolutionise DR screening and management, enhancing access, financial sustainability and the precision of care.53
Economic assessments reveal the cost-effectiveness of telehealth screening, demonstrating significant savings compared with conventional in-person screenings and underscoring the financial viability of telehealth initiatives.114 Additionally, employing telehealth physician extenders for re-engagement strategies has shown to significantly augment the return rate of patients for eye care, suggesting a scalable model to enhance patient adherence in DR management.115
The advent of explainable AI-enabled teleophthalmology for DR grading and classification marks a significant technological leap. Such innovations hold the promise of affordable, continuous disease monitoring, potentially ameliorating treatment outcomes and patient care, especially in remote or underserved regions.116
In synthesis, the incorporation of technological innovations and telemedicine offers new vistas and methodologies in the domain of DR management. By harnessing these advanced technological means, there is an unparalleled potential not only to augment the rates of early diagnosis and treatment efficacy for DR but also to afford patients more personalised, convenient and efficient medical services. As these technologies persist in evolving, their application and influence in DR management are anticipated to widen, engendering greater optimism and opportunities for DR patients. This trajectory underscores a future where telehealth services play a central role in reshaping the landscape of DR management, signifying a shift towards more accessible, effective and patient-centric healthcare solutions.

Ethical challenges and social impact in DR medical management
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Addressing the ethical challenges of DR treatments and their social impact requires a multifaceted approach that encompasses the safety and effectiveness of emerging treatment methods, as well as their ethical rationale and real-world impact on patients’ quality of life. The introduction of advanced technologies, such as gene editing and stem cell therapy, into DR treatments introduces several ethical dilemmas. The uncertainty surrounding the long-term effects of gene therapy and societal and ethical concerns regarding potential genetic modifications are prime examples of the complex issues at play. Furthermore, the use of embryonic stem cells in stem cell therapy ignites ethical debates regarding the source of these cells.100 As a result, new DR treatments must be subject to thorough ethical reviews to ensure adherence to moral standards and respect for patients’ autonomy and interests.50 64
The ethical landscape is further complicated by the social implications of technological advancements in healthcare. High costs associated with cutting-edge DR treatments could worsen social inequalities, limiting access for economically disadvantaged patients. Additionally, the rapid adoption of novel technologies raises public concerns about privacy and data security, underscoring the need for a comprehensive evaluation of these technologies’ social impacts. Implementing policies and adjusting medical insurance coverage are crucial steps towards promoting equitable access to these innovations, while safeguarding patient privacy and data protection.
Incorporating recent insights into the discussion, Bryl et al and Tan and Wong highlight the importance of considering non-pharmacological interventions and the ethical implications of deploying AI in healthcare, respectively.117 118 These considerations emphasise the need to balance technological innovation with ethical principles and social equity. Similarly, the work of Kulkarni et al and Mounirou et al sheds light on the role of social determinants in health outcomes and the comprehensive overview of DR treatments, advocating for patient-centred care that addresses both medical and quality-of-life concerns.63 119
In conclusion, the management of DR transcends medical boundaries to encompass ethical and social dimensions. It calls for a concerted effort among healthcare professionals, researchers, policy-makers and the broader community to navigate these challenges effectively. Ensuring that technological advancements in DR treatment are ethically sound and socially equitable is paramount to improving care outcomes for all patients affected by this condition. This integrated approach highlights the critical need for ethical scrutiny, social awareness and collaborative solutions in the evolving landscape of DR management.

STUDY LIMITATIONS
-----------------
While this review provides a comprehensive overview of DR management, several limitations must be acknowledged. First, this research predominantly focuses on English-language literature published within the past decade, which may overlook significant findings from non-English sources. Although searches were conducted across multiple databases—including PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science and the Cochrane Library—other potentially relevant databases, such as Embase, were not included, potentially leading to the omission of pertinent studies.
Second, as a narrative review, this article does not perform a systematic meta-analysis. While it synthesises existing research to offer a broad perspective, it lacks the quantitative analysis necessary to assess the effects and consistency of different studies. Furthermore, narrative reviews may introduce subjective biases in literature selection and data integration, impacting the objectivity of the findings.
Third, although this review discusses the application of emerging technologies such as telemedicine, AI and gene therapy in DR management, these innovations remain in the early stages of clinical implementation. The absence of large-scale, long-term follow-up data limits the conclusions drawn regarding their effectiveness and feasibility in widespread use, rendering them somewhat speculative.
Finally, the review primarily draws from global DR research. While it addresses challenges and strategies relevant to resource-limited settings, the discussion regarding specific populations—particularly those in low-income countries or distinct racial/ethnic groups—may lack sufficient depth. Consequently, the applicability and feasibility of the proposed strategies require further exploration in diverse regional contexts. In conclusion, future research should strive for a more inclusive integration of non-English literature, undertake systematic meta-analyses and conduct long-term clinical validations of emerging technologies, which are essential for providing more comprehensive and reliable evidence in the management of diabetes retinopathy.

CONCLUSIONS
-----------
This study underscores the urgent need for personalised treatment plans, comprehensive patient education, and a holistic approach to managing DR. The investigation into novel therapies—including innovative pharmacological agents, gene and stem cell therapies, as well as the integration of AI and telemedicine—has the potential to transform the diagnostic and therapeutic landscape for DR.
In summary, this article advocates for global collaboration and multidisciplinary efforts, emphasising the importance of innovative management strategies, particularly in resource-limited settings. Such initiatives aim to develop new methods for mitigating vision loss associated with DR. By promoting health equity and enhancing treatment adherence, these strategies seek to improve outcomes for diabetic patients and guide future research towards minimising the public health impact of DR. A concerted global effort is essential for advancing the management of DR and alleviating its burden on public health systems worldwide.
