Future directions and research prospects In the field of medical management of DR, ongoing innovation and research continue to break new ground in treatment modalities, personalised medical strategies and the use of technological advances to improve the efficiency of patient management. Here are some important future directions in this field: Research advances in new treatments As medical research deepens, the treatment landscape for DR is rapidly evolving, particularly in the exploration of new therapeutic approaches. These aim not only to control disease progression but also to address the underlying causes of the disease, thereby improving patients’ potential for visual recovery and quality of life. Scientists are exploring multiple treatment targets based on the pathological mechanisms of DR. In addition to the widely used anti-VEGF therapies, new research is focusing on key biological pathways such as fibroblast growth factor, platelet-derived growth factor and the Wnt/β-catenin pathway. These are aimed at reducing retinal vascular leakage and abnormal neovascularisation to comprehensively address the visual impairment caused by DR.96 97 Gene therapy and stem cell therapy, as revolutionary fields for the treatment of DR, show immense potential. The former aims to achieve curative treatment by correcting pathogenic genes, while the latter harnesses the regenerative potential of stem cells to repair damaged retinal cells.98 99 However, the safety, efficacy and long-term effects of these treatments need to be validated through extensive clinical trials. Personalised medicine plays an increasingly important role in selecting treatment strategies, which should be optimised based on the patient’s genetic background, disease state and personal needs. In summary, emerging treatment methods for DR are opening new doors for more in-depth and personalised treatment. As scientific research progresses, the future promises more effective, safe and personalised treatment options for DR patients. Personalised medicine in diabetes retinopathy management Personalised medicine, often referred to as precision medicine, customises treatment plans based on an individual’s genetic profile, lifestyle and specific disease characteristics. In the context of DR management, this approach leverages genetic markers to assess the risk of disease progression and predict responses to treatment, thereby facilitating more tailored and effective therapeutic strategies for patients.100 By adjusting drug dosages and treatment schedules according to a patient’s unique disease state and responses, personalised medicine aims to maximise treatment efficacy while minimising adverse effects.101 The integration of personalised medicine into DR management represents a significant advancement in medical treatment, particularly within the framework of precision medicine. By considering individual genetic backgrounds, biomarkers, environmental factors and lifestyle choices, customised treatment strategies can be developed for each DR patient. Current research has identified several genetic markers that correlate with both the risk of developing DR and the efficacy of various treatments. Notably, specific genetic variations, such as polymorphisms in the VEGF gene, have been associated with disease progression and response to anti-VEGF therapies. Genetic analysis can effectively identify patients more likely to benefit from anti-VEGF treatments, enabling the formulation of personalised treatment regimens.102 Moreover, strategies that target inflammatory and oxidative stress pathways are being explored, reflecting the latest advancements in understanding these mechanisms.103 Personalised treatment extends beyond genetic considerations; it also encompasses tailored plans based on individual patient circumstances, including the duration of diabetes, the presence of comorbidities and lifestyle factors. For example, individualised management of glycosylated hemoglobin, type A1C (HbA1c) levels, blood pressure and lipid profiles is essential in the context of DR. These clinical characteristics significantly influence the selection of optimal treatment modalities, such as the frequency of anti-VEGF injections, the incorporation of combination therapies (eg, laser photocoagulation alongside pharmacotherapy) and lifestyle modifications.41 Adjustments to the dosage and injection intervals of anti-VEGF therapy are critical, based on the patient’s response to treatment. While some patients may achieve satisfactory outcomes with standard dosing regimens, others might require more frequent injections or higher doses to effectively manage disease progression.104 Continuous monitoring of disease status through repeated retinal imaging techniques, such as OCT, is vital for optimising individualised treatment plans. In conclusion, the application of personalised medicine in the management of DR highlights the potential of precision medicine to improve treatment outcomes and enhance patient experiences. As research continues to advance and technologies evolve, personalised medicine is poised to play an increasingly pivotal role in the management of DR and other related conditions, ultimately striving to preserve vision and enhance quality of life for patients.14 Technological innovations and the potential of telemedicine With the rapid advancement of technology, telemedicine has become an important innovation in the management of DR. In particular, with the support of digital health technologies and AI, unprecedented opportunities have been opened up for early screening, monitoring and intervention of DR.105 106 These technologies not only meet patients’ demands for convenient and efficient medical services but also open new avenues for healthcare providers to deliver high-quality eye care in resource-limited settings. Digital retinal imaging technology Digital retinal imaging technology enables the remote transmission of retinal photographs, allowing doctors to assess patients without direct face-to-face interaction. The use of this technology significantly increases the efficiency of DR screening programmes, allowing patients in remote areas to access timely DR screening services.107 Compared with traditional fundus examination, digital retinal imaging technology provides higher-quality images, which helps clinicians to more accurately analyse and diagnose lesions. Application of AI in diabetes retinopathy diagnosis AI, particularly through the use of deep learning algorithms, has demonstrated remarkable potential in the automatic detection, classification and analysis of DR images.108 109 These AI systems can efficiently process vast quantities of retinal image data, accurately identifying subtle lesions that may elude even the most experienced clinicians. This capability positions AI as a crucial adjunctive tool, enabling ophthalmologists to detect DR at earlier stages and facilitating timely intervention. When comparing AI to traditional screening methods, such as manual assessments conducted by ophthalmologists, AI has shown superior sensitivity, specificity, speed and scalability. Numerous studies indicate that AI technologies have achieved diagnostic accuracies comparable to, or exceeding, those of certain human experts.81 110 This advancement underscores the value of AI in enhancing the effectiveness and efficiency of DR screening programmes. Despite the promising applications of AI in clinical settings, several challenges must be addressed, including concerns regarding data privacy, algorithmic bias, regulatory compliance and the need for widespread adoption in practical environments.111 Tackling these issues is essential for the successful integration of AI into routine clinical practice. Moreover, the future trajectory of AI in DR management will likely involve greater integration with telemedicine platforms, thereby providing convenient DR screening services for patients in remote or underserved regions. In summary, the incorporation of digital retinal imaging technologies alongside AI in the management of DR holds significant promise for enhancing early detection and intervention, especially in areas with limited access to healthcare. As these technologies advance and existing challenges are systematically addressed, they are expected to revolutionise the landscape of DR care, delivering more personalised and accessible healthcare solutions. The potential of telehealth services The potential of telehealth services in managing DR is increasingly recognised as a pivotal component in enhancing patient care. Telemedicine services, facilitating online consultations, disease management advice and patient education, significantly bolster the self-management capacities of DR patients, offering a blend of convenience, efficiency and personalised care.112 These services not only mitigate the time and economic burdens on patients but also foster their active engagement and satisfaction in managing their condition. Furthermore, telemedicine’s capability for continuous disease monitoring emerges as a critical benefit, especially for those hindered by geographical remoteness or physical limitations from regular hospital visits. Recent advancements further extend the scope and efficacy of telehealth in DR management. The third edition of the Practice Guidelines for Ocular Telehealth-Diabetic Retinopathy underscores an evolving framework to refine telehealth’s clinical applications in ophthalmology, emphasising standardised protocols for image acquisition and evaluation.113 Concurrently, the integration of AI within telehealth frameworks is poised to revolutionise DR screening and management, enhancing access, financial sustainability and the precision of care.53 Economic assessments reveal the cost-effectiveness of telehealth screening, demonstrating significant savings compared with conventional in-person screenings and underscoring the financial viability of telehealth initiatives.114 Additionally, employing telehealth physician extenders for re-engagement strategies has shown to significantly augment the return rate of patients for eye care, suggesting a scalable model to enhance patient adherence in DR management.115 The advent of explainable AI-enabled teleophthalmology for DR grading and classification marks a significant technological leap. Such innovations hold the promise of affordable, continuous disease monitoring, potentially ameliorating treatment outcomes and patient care, especially in remote or underserved regions.116 In synthesis, the incorporation of technological innovations and telemedicine offers new vistas and methodologies in the domain of DR management. By harnessing these advanced technological means, there is an unparalleled potential not only to augment the rates of early diagnosis and treatment efficacy for DR but also to afford patients more personalised, convenient and efficient medical services. As these technologies persist in evolving, their application and influence in DR management are anticipated to widen, engendering greater optimism and opportunities for DR patients. This trajectory underscores a future where telehealth services play a central role in reshaping the landscape of DR management, signifying a shift towards more accessible, effective and patient-centric healthcare solutions. Ethical challenges and social impact in DR medical management Addressing the ethical challenges of DR treatments and their social impact requires a multifaceted approach that encompasses the safety and effectiveness of emerging treatment methods, as well as their ethical rationale and real-world impact on patients’ quality of life. The introduction of advanced technologies, such as gene editing and stem cell therapy, into DR treatments introduces several ethical dilemmas. The uncertainty surrounding the long-term effects of gene therapy and societal and ethical concerns regarding potential genetic modifications are prime examples of the complex issues at play. Furthermore, the use of embryonic stem cells in stem cell therapy ignites ethical debates regarding the source of these cells.100 As a result, new DR treatments must be subject to thorough ethical reviews to ensure adherence to moral standards and respect for patients’ autonomy and interests.50 64 The ethical landscape is further complicated by the social implications of technological advancements in healthcare. High costs associated with cutting-edge DR treatments could worsen social inequalities, limiting access for economically disadvantaged patients. Additionally, the rapid adoption of novel technologies raises public concerns about privacy and data security, underscoring the need for a comprehensive evaluation of these technologies’ social impacts. Implementing policies and adjusting medical insurance coverage are crucial steps towards promoting equitable access to these innovations, while safeguarding patient privacy and data protection. Incorporating recent insights into the discussion, Bryl et al and Tan and Wong highlight the importance of considering non-pharmacological interventions and the ethical implications of deploying AI in healthcare, respectively.117 118 These considerations emphasise the need to balance technological innovation with ethical principles and social equity. Similarly, the work of Kulkarni et al and Mounirou et al sheds light on the role of social determinants in health outcomes and the comprehensive overview of DR treatments, advocating for patient-centred care that addresses both medical and quality-of-life concerns.63 119 In conclusion, the management of DR transcends medical boundaries to encompass ethical and social dimensions. It calls for a concerted effort among healthcare professionals, researchers, policy-makers and the broader community to navigate these challenges effectively. Ensuring that technological advancements in DR treatment are ethically sound and socially equitable is paramount to improving care outcomes for all patients affected by this condition. This integrated approach highlights the critical need for ethical scrutiny, social awareness and collaborative solutions in the evolving landscape of DR management.
